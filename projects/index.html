CHI 2026 paper · project page
Interactive Storybooks for Early AI Literacy

A series of interactive digital storybooks that teach children ages 6-9 how AI works. Kids follow Doodlebot, a robot who dreams of becoming an artist, through mini-games and hands-on creative AI activities.

In a study with 57 children, the storybook version led to significantly greater learning gains than the same activities without the story (CHI 2026). A follow-up on multiple pathways to early AI literacy will appear at EAAI 2027. This project was also my M.S. thesis.

Doodlebot

a drawing robot for K-12 AI education, and a lens on how children think about robot authority

HRI 2026 paper · RO-MAN 2026 paper
Doodlebot

Doodlebot is an educational robot from the Personal Robots Group that children program with blocks to draw, dance, express emotions, and use AI.

With Doodlebot, we studied how children ages 6-9 think robots should fit into everyday authority. Children resisted robot control in areas tied to their own skills and self-expression, and expected teacher, classmate, and mentee robots to behave differently (HRI 2026). We also found that changing a robot’s voice, decorations, or costume shapes the traits and classroom roles children give it, but not whether they would listen to it (RO-MAN 2026).

EmoToon

a human-AI co-creativity tool to design emotional comic storyboards for non-artists

ACII 2026 paper
EmoToon

EmoToon helps non-artists turn their feelings into sketch-style comic storyboards with the help of generative AI.

In a study with 25 participants, AI assistance improved emotional expression, aesthetic quality, and exploration, but reduced people’s sense of creative ownership. Led with Jocelyn Shen (ACII 2026).

IDC 2026 paper · play the game
Orbiting the VirtueVerse

A game where middle school students practice ethical decision-making about AI through scenarios grounded in virtue ethics.

In a workshop with 22 students ages 13-14, students learned about virtue ethics, played the game, and co-designed new scenarios for future versions. Led with Daniella DiPaola (IDC 2026).

A HeARTfelt Robot

AI-powered social robot guided art discussion for social emotional learning in kids

RO-MAN 2024 paper
A HeARTfelt Robot

Jibo, a social robot powered by GPT-4, guides children ages 7-11 through conversations about art to practice social-emotional learning.

Children showed more empathetic reasoning when discussing emotional art than neutral art, and Jibo’s responses helped ease their discomfort after sharing vulnerable feelings. This work won the RSJ Pioneering Research Award at RO-MAN 2024.

As an MIT SERC Scholar, I worked with Dr. Anastasia Ostrowski on participatory design of generative AI for high school education.

Games for Learning How LLMs Work

CHI 2026 workshop paper
Games for Learning How LLMs Work

Two games that demonstrate the principles behind large language models. In Learn Like an LLM, players see how LLMs are trained to predict text from a dataset. In Tag-Team Text Generation, players generate text one word at a time using predicted probabilities and randomness.

With Allison Chen (CHI 2026 Workshop on Data Literacy).

Telling Climate Stories with Data

With Matt Taylor and Rachel Connolly, I developed a week-long curriculum where high schoolers analyze climate data in Python and look for the story in their data.

Students learn to clean, visualize, and build a predictive machine learning model with their data, then present it as a piece of data theatre.

Robotic Percussion Quartet

co-designed a robotic percussion quartet system with musicians to play uniquely robotic music with human interaction

HRI 2025 paper · performance video · thesis talk
Robotic Percussion Quartet

For my senior thesis at Princeton, I co-designed the Beatbots, a quartet of percussion robots, with musicians.

Instead of imitating human players, the Beatbots lean into what robots do well (precise timing, randomness, and tight coordination) to make music that is hard for humans to play. Audience members can join in through a MIDI keyboard. Published at HRI 2025.

GANs Animations

exhibit
GANs Animations

For my junior independent work at Princeton, advised by Prof. Adam Finkelstein, I created two p5.js animations explaining GANs for The Imitation Game exhibit at the Vancouver Art Gallery (March-October 2022).

I trained several GANs to make the animation assets. Over 85% of surveyed viewers reported learning a lot about GANs.
